# Task 5 – Audience Rating Insights Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project analyzes Netflix titles by audience rating and content type (Movies and TV Shows). The aim is to understand how titles are distributed across rating categories, compare the rating profiles of Movies and TV Shows, and present the findings in an executive-style Excel report.

## Task Description

**Task 5 (Advanced) – Audience Rating Insights Dashboard**

Develop an executive dashboard analyzing audience ratings and content categories.

## Task Workflow

1. **Analyze rating categories** – Review the audience-rating field and identify the categories represented in the dataset.
2. **Build KPI indicators** – Establish useful headline measures, including total titles, Movies, TV Shows, and the most common rating.
3. **Create interactive visual reports** – Use Excel charts to communicate the distribution of ratings and compare content types.
4. **Add slicers for filtering** – Use slicers to filter the report and explore the data interactively.
5. **Deliver audience insights** – Summarize the key findings and explain how they may support content and audience decisions.

## Dataset

The analysis uses a Netflix titles dataset containing **8,790 titles**:
- **6,126 Movies** (approximately 69.7%)
- **2,664 TV Shows** (approximately 30.3%)

The audience-rating field is used to compare how titles are distributed across rating categories and content types.

## The Three Basic Data Analyst Questions

### 1. What did I analyze, and what question was I trying to answer?

I analyzed Netflix titles by audience rating and content type. The main question was: **How are titles distributed across audience-rating categories, and how do the rating profiles of Movies and TV Shows differ?**

### 2. What specific finding did the analysis reveal?

TV-MA was the most common rating, with 3,205 titles, followed by TV-14 with 2,157 titles. Together, these two categories account for approximately 61% of all titles in the dataset. The analysis also showed that TV-Y and TV-Y7 have higher TV Show counts than Movie counts.

### 3. What action could the findings support?

The findings can support content categorization, age-appropriate content discovery, and audience segmentation. The comparison between Movies and TV Shows can also help decision-makers examine whether the catalogue's content mix aligns with their programming objectives.

## Business Interpretation

The large share of TV-MA and TV-14 titles indicates that mature-audience and teen-audience ratings make up a substantial portion of the catalogue represented in this dataset. Meanwhile, the higher TV Show counts in the TV-Y and TV-Y7 categories highlight a different distribution for child-oriented content.

These insights can guide further investigation into catalogue balance, content classification, and audience-focused programming. However, title counts do **not** measure viewing figures, audience preference, popularity, revenue, or demand. Those conclusions would require additional performance and audience data.
